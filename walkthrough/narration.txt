Port City FC’s challenge was not a lack of information. It was that financial records, operating assumptions, staffing coverage, and matchday decisions were disconnected. The result was a familiar business-analysis problem: leaders could see activity, but not a governed line from evidence to decision.

I built this intelligence hub to close that gap. The opening Ownership Snapshot gives a stakeholder one screen for financial position, operating drivers, evidence quality, and the decisions that need an owner. In the sanitized portfolio model, income is sixty-seven thousand five hundred dollars, spend is sixty-three thousand seven hundred, and modeled net cash is three thousand eight hundred. Those figures are synthetic, and the interface says so clearly.

That evidence boundary is deliberate. The public repository contains no bank rows, payees, staff names, contact details, private source filenames, or attributed interview text. Sensitive inputs were replaced with generalized assumptions and deterministic source files. A reproducible pipeline builds the model and produces seven reconciliation checks with zero current differences. This separates a credible analytical method from an unsafe claim that portfolio data is audited club performance.

The most important modeled finding is concentration. Sponsorship represents just over fifty-six percent of income, while the cash margin is only five point six percent. Together, those measures suggest a decision: protect renewal ownership and set a minimum liquidity threshold before approving growth assumptions. A second evidence gap is equally important. The public evidence does not establish the home-and-away fixture split, so the hub refuses to present per-home-match projections as an approved forecast.

The Scenario Lab demonstrates that control. It is an illustrative sensitivity workspace with transparent formulas and clearly labeled assumptions. In the public demonstration it is read-only. In the full local workflow, an authorized user can create, update, or delete a scenario through a PostgreSQL transaction. Every write records the actor, organization, entity, action, and before-and-after payload in the audit log. Route-level authorization and entity-specific permissions are enforced on the server, not merely hidden in the interface.

The architecture follows the same principle from end to end: sanitized CSV sources, a reproducible transformation pipeline, reconciled outputs, a star-schema Power BI companion with DAX measures and row-level security roles, and a Next.js decision interface backed by tested policy guards. Continuous integration runs privacy, lineage, model, formatting, lint, type, unit, integration, build, database, and browser checks.

The case study documents the business problem, stakeholders, requirements, exclusions, traceability, acceptance tests, findings, and roadmap. That documentation matters because a professional dashboard is more than attractive charts. It must show where a number came from, who may act on it, which decision it supports, and what remains uncertain.

The next production step is controlled replacement of the synthetic model: approve source ownership, map the live systems, validate fixture and attendance definitions, assign Power BI service roles, and complete stakeholder UAT. The portfolio proves the architecture and analytical discipline without exposing the club’s private data.
