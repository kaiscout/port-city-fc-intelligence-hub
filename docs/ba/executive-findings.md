# Executive Findings and Recommendations

## Findings

1. **Retention is the compounding lever.** Improving repeat attendance raises revenue without reacquiring every supporter and strengthens sponsor audience quality.
2. **Ticket-start conversion is a practical constraint.** The demonstration funnel indicates a high-intent handoff worth optimizing before broad reach expansion.
3. **Sponsor delivery is revenue protection.** Overdue or weakly evidenced deliverables create renewal risk that can be managed early with visible ownership.
4. **Women’s-program growth is a commercial and community opportunity.** The model should receive equivalent operational quality, program-specific measures, and investment gates—not a residual allocation.
5. **Shared costs change the story.** Explicit allocation prevents one program absorbing common overhead and supports credible decisions.
6. **Provenance is part of product quality.** A number without source class, owner, and method is not executive evidence.

## Recommendations

- Approve a 90-day parallel-run pilot with manual/CSV data and no external writes.
- Establish target definitions and source owners before loading real records.
- Use a weekly fixture economics and sponsor-exception review.
- Fund the women’s baseline growth case; release the next tranche only after conversion, sponsor, and operational gates.
- Replace estimated reach with provider-validated methodology before external use.
- Complete security/privacy/retention/backup approval before production hosting.
- Consider integrations only after the manual boundary is stable and reconciliation effort is measured.
